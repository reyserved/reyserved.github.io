## Service Credit Union

**Case:** Building and measuring an AI-answer visibility program
**Role:** Led strategy and page audits
**Period:** Jul 12 – Sep 7, 2026 (Semrush); August 2026 (GSC)
**Sources:** Semrush AI Visibility; GSC Generative AI Features

### What the evidence supports

- Page-level citation breadth increased for several product and membership pages.
- AI Overview was Service Credit Union's strongest AI surface in the measured windows.
- The auto-loan hub showed both AI feature visibility and high citation counts in two independent sources.
- A high-intent preapproval guide earned more clicks on nearly flat impressions.

### What it does not support

- Overall AI Visibility stayed at 17/100 against a 44 target; this is not a completed GEO win.
- No clicks, calls, or leads are attributed to AI surfaces in the source data.
- The vehicle-loans hub declined from 65 to 49 cited prompts, so gains were not uniform.
- The two Semrush windows overlap by three days; changes are directional, not exact.
- Page-level GSC AI-feature impression shares are withheld pending reconciliation of the source export.

### Likely follow-up questions

**Why was AI Visibility only 17/100 if citations increased?**

AI Visibility is a brand-level score across platforms; the gains were page-level, and AI Overview was the strongest surface. The 17/100 vs 44 target is a limitation, not a win I hide.

**Can you prove AI citations drove leads?**

No. The source data has no click, call, or lead attribution from AI surfaces. The next step would be GA4 AI referral segments and CRM attribution.

**Why did the vehicle-loans hub decline?**

It moved from 65 to 49 cited prompts. Citation breadth is not uniform, and the two Semrush windows overlap by three days, so the change is directional.

**What would you do next?**

Isolate AI referrals, refresh stale statistics and source links, close FAQ/schema gaps, and track citations monthly.

## TDE Family Law

**Case:** An answer-first content system for a family law firm
**Role:** Led content and local strategy
**Period:** Mar 7 – Apr 3, 2026; vs Feb 7 – Mar 6, 2026
**Sources:** GSC, GA4, CallRail, GBP; AI Overview content map

### What the evidence supports

- Business-relevant search exposure grew after removing celebrity and news distortion.
- A single content page earned substantially more clicks through better snippet performance.
- Local search demand produced more calls and more first-time callers.
- The query set has significant AI Overview exposure, making answer-first structure commercially relevant.

### What it does not support

- Branded search carried most of the click growth; this is not proof of non-brand click growth.
- Qualified Website Pool calls stayed flat at 2 and qualified GBP calls fell from 24 to 16.
- The prenup result is a small base of 14 clicks and should not be generalized to the whole blog.
- Homepage CTR fell from 2.26% to 1.69% as impressions broadened.
- GA4 engagement figures are all-users, not organic-only.

### Likely follow-up questions

**Did non-brand clicks grow?**

Branded search carried most of the click growth. The defensible claim is filtered commercial impressions +19.5% and query clicks +14.5%, not non-brand clicks.

**Did qualified leads improve?**

No. Qualified Website Pool calls stayed flat at 2, and qualified GBP calls fell from 24 to 16. The result is demand, not lead quality.

**Was the prenup page a ranking win?**

No. Clicks went 4 to 14 and CTR 0.39% to 1.04% while average position slipped from 11.91 to 13.0. It is a snippet and intent-match win.

**How did you handle the celebrity/news distortion?**

I measured business-relevant queries and pages with celebrity and low-quality traffic excluded, and reported the filtered change rather than raw sitewide totals.

## Dream Assurance Group

**Case:** Migration-led growth for an independent insurance agency
**Role:** Part-time, project-to-project<br>Dec 2023 – Dec 2025
**Period:** Migration and optimization through Dec 2025; GSC results Sep 2025 – Mar 2026
**Sources:** Google Search Console; Semrush keyword export, schema validators, GTM/GA4

### What the evidence supports

- Organic clicks and impressions grew materially after the migration and optimization work.
- The growth continued into the following quarter rather than being a one-month spike.
- The site has extensive AI Overview exposure, making answer-first structure commercially relevant.
- The program combined migration, architecture, schema, content, and measurement under one owner.

### What it does not support

- CTR fell from 0.27% to 0.20% as impressions broadened; this is not a CTR win.
- GSC cannot attribute growth to a single change; this is migration-led evidence, not proof that every tactic worked.
- The February 2026 keyword snapshot is a point-in-time export, not a growth metric.
- The Semrush trend has no quantified values for April–November 2026 in the source screenshots.
- AI Overview presence is not a citation; no clean before-and-after AI citation trend is available for Dream Assurance.

### Likely follow-up questions

**Did your work cause the growth?**

The migration, architecture, brand, and answer-first work preceded the Dec 2025–Feb 2026 growth, but GSC cannot attribute it to a single change. It is migration-led evidence, not proof that every tactic worked.

**Why does the AI Overview trend stop at March 2026?**

The Semrush screenshots have no quantified values for April–November 2026. I only chart what the source supports.

**What happened to CTR?**

It fell from 0.27% to 0.20% as impressions broadened. It is not a CTR win, and I would not present it as one.

**Is AI Overview presence a citation?**

No. Presence is not a citation, and there is no clean before-and-after AI citation trend for Dream Assurance.

**What was your role and engagement?**

Part-time, project-to-project, Dec 2023 to Dec 2025. I owned the migration, information architecture, brand intelligence, answer-first content and schema, and GTM/GA4 tracking.

## General guidance

- Lead with the role and the period before the result.
- State the source and the period with every metric.
- If asked for a limitation, give one specific limitation, not a generic caveat.
- Never convert a visibility metric into a lead or revenue claim.
- For any "what would you do next" question, name the missing measurement first.
