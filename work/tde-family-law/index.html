An answer-first content system for a family law firm

Mapping 151 family-law queries to AI Overview exposure, then rebuilding content so each page answers the query in its first paragraph.

01 · Objective

Grow commercially relevant search demand, not vanity traffic

TDE Family Law needed content that would attract people actively researching divorce, custody, support, and prenuptial questions in Georgia, and turn that visibility into phone demand.

The measurement problem came first: raw sitewide totals were distorted by celebrity and news traffic, so the program had to be judged on business-relevant queries, not headline numbers.

02 · Insight

Two-thirds of the tracked query set already triggered AI Overviews

The content map flagged 67 of 151 tracked terms as AI Overview queries. That made answer extraction a first-order requirement, not an experiment.

After filtering out celebrity and news noise, query impressions rose 19.5% and query clicks 14.5%, while the homepage's average position improved from 18.05 to 13.68.

03 · Strategy

Answer-first briefs, question-led pages, and local demand capture

The strategy combined AEO content structure with local SEO:

  • Map each priority query to the page that should own the answer.
  • Open with a direct definition or answer before expanding into detail.
  • Structure follow-ups as question headings with concise, citable answers.
  • Cite authoritative sources for legal statements rather than asserting them.
  • Strengthen Google Business Profile and location signals to convert demand into calls.
04 · Execution

Content map, briefs, page optimization, and GBP work

I led the content and local strategy: building the AI Overview content map, shaping briefs around answer-first structure, and optimizing pages for snippet and intent match.

The prenup guide is the clearest example. Clicks went from 4 to 14 and CTR from 0.39% to 1.04% while average position slipped from 11.91 to 13.0. That is a snippet and intent-match gain, not a ranking jump.

What this proves, and what it does not

What the evidence supports

  • Business-relevant search exposure grew after removing celebrity and news distortion.
  • A single content page earned substantially more clicks through better snippet performance.
  • Local search demand produced more calls and more first-time callers.
  • The query set has significant AI Overview exposure, making answer-first structure commercially relevant.

What it does not support

  • Branded search carried most of the click growth; this is not proof of non-brand click growth.
  • Qualified Website Pool calls stayed flat at 2 and qualified GBP calls fell from 24 to 16.
  • The prenup result is a small base of 14 clicks and should not be generalized to the whole blog.
  • Homepage CTR fell from 2.26% to 1.69% as impressions broadened.
  • GA4 engagement figures are all-users, not organic-only.
